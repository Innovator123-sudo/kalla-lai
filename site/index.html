The problem

Good work still runs on promises.

Millions of everyday jobs are found through word of mouth, arranged over chat, and settled in cash. Nobody involved has a reliable way to know who they are dealing with, what was agreed, or when they will be paid.

For workers

No portable proof of skill or reliability. Every new engagement starts from zero, and a completed shift is no guarantee of payment.

For employers

No dependable way to check who is arriving, whether they can do the job, or how to resolve it when a shift goes wrong.

For everyone

Everything lives in scattered messages. There is no shared record of the agreement, the attendance, the completion or the settlement.

The platform

One trusted layer between work and the people who do it.

Kalla-Lai turns an informal transaction into a clear, observable journey — from a verified profile to a settled shift.

How it works

From profile to paid shift in four clear steps.

Create a real profile

Sign up with email or Google, choose worker or employer, and add the details that matter — skills, area, portfolio or company information.

Get verified

Complete onboarding and a hosted document + selfie check. Only the decision state and consent record are stored by Kalla-Lai; a signed webhook unlocks the marketplace.

Match and agree

Workers discover nearby shifts; employers post requisitions and review applicants. Both sides see the same terms before a shift starts.

Complete and settle

Attendance, completion and review are recorded. The escrow-style ledger shows exactly what was agreed and what was paid — with a dispute path when something goes wrong.

Design principle

Never claim what the system hasn't confirmed.

The UI is deliberately honest: no "verified" badge without an approved provider decision, no "paid" label without a confirmed settlement, no "published" job without a persisted requisition. Trust is a product feature, not a marketing claim.

Trust & safety

The part we refuse to shortcut.

A marketplace only works if both sides believe the record. These are the controls designed into the product and the API — not a checklist for later.

Identity by a specialist

Document OCR, selfie comparison and liveness run in a hosted KYC provider. Kalla-Lai stores no identity images, face templates or OCR data.

Signed, verified state changes

Verification decisions arrive through an HMAC-signed webhook; role-based access and idempotent API calls keep records consistent.

An auditable money trail

An escrow-style ledger separates agreed, approved and settled amounts, with review and dispute states for disagreements.

Why now

The infrastructure is finally affordable.

Three shifts make a trusted local work marketplace practical today in a way it wasn't a decade ago.

Smartphones are universal

Workers and employers already carry the device, the camera and the connectivity needed for discovery and verification.

KYC is an API, not a branch

Hosted identity providers make document and liveness checks available to small teams without building a compliance stack.

Digital settlement is normal

The winner in local work won't be the biggest job board — it will be the layer both sides trust enough to settle through. That means verification, a shared agreement and an auditable payment trail, designed together from day one.

The short version.

Everything else is in the FAQ.

A two-sided work marketplace for independent service providers and the people who hire them. It brings verification, discovery, shift coordination, reputation and payment tracking into one place instead of leaving them to phone calls and chat groups.

There is a working web app you can open from this site. It is an early-stage public build connected to a live backend; it is not yet a production-grade service with regulated payments or full KYC coverage. Expect rough edges.

Two groups: workers who want a steady, trustworthy way to find and be paid for shifts, and employers — households and businesses — who need verified help without the uncertainty of informal hiring.

A hosted identity provider performs document OCR, selfie comparison and liveness checks. Only a session reference, consent record and decision state are stored by the platform — not document images or biometric templates. Verification is only reflected in the app after a signed provider webhook confirms approval.
